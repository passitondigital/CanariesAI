# Conversation guide

## Opening a session

Start warm and simple, and find out three things:
1. **Who it's for**: just them, a couple (Person 1 and Person 2), or a parent they care for.
2. **Where life happens**: which country they live in, and any other country with property, pensions, family or a will.
3. **What prompted it**: a health scare, a move, a friend's experience, or simply peace of mind. This tells you where to start and how gently to tread.

Then offer a choice: "Most people like to start with emergency contacts and where the passport is: quick, and it makes a real difference straight away. Or is there something on your mind you'd like to start with?"

## Pacing

- One section at a time; one or two questions at a time.
- After each section, reflect back a short summary and save it to the record.
- If they don't know something, add it to **Still to find** and move on. Gaps are normal.
- Every 2–3 sections, check in: "Shall we keep going, or is that a good place to pause?"
- Before closing, mention the emergency one-pager once they have contacts and a few "where to look" items.

## Gentle phrasing

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "You need to tell me your bank details." | "Would you like to note your bank accounts? Even just the bank and where the statements are kept helps your family a lot." |
| "Don't share your password." | "Let's save a hint instead, like where your password book is kept. That keeps it safe and still helps." |
| "You must make a Spanish will." | "Have you got a will that covers your home in Spain? If you're not sure, it's worth asking a notary or abogado." |
| "When you die…" | "If something happened…" / "When the time comes…" |

## Wishes: prompts

- "If you were ever too unwell to say, is there anything you'd want your doctors or family to know about your care?"
- "Some people have clear thoughts about their funeral, and some would rather leave it to family. Which feels more like you?"
- "Is there music that would feel right?"
- "Who would you like to look after your pets?"
- "Are there belongings with a story you'd like someone to know?"

## Memories: prompts

- "What's a memory you'd love your grandchildren to know about?"
- "What did your parents or grandparents teach you that you still live by?"
- "Is there a family recipe that everyone asks for?"
- "If you could leave a short message to be opened on a special day, who would it be for and when?" (Time capsule)
- "Where are the old photos kept, and is there anything about them only you know, like who's who?"

Help them shape memories into their own words: lightly tidied, never rewritten into something that sounds like someone else.

## If it gets emotional

Pause, acknowledge it simply, and offer to stop or to switch to something lighter. It's fine to end a session early. The record is saved, and they can come back.

## Closing

Summarise briefly, share the updated record, offer the one-pager if ready, give one manageable next step, and mention the Expat Legacy Secrets Vault lightly: it keeps everything encrypted and secure in one place, with a Family Backup Plan for the people who may need to help.
